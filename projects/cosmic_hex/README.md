# Cosmic Hex

An original space game of exploring, settling and trading on a hex board, for 2 to 4 players: you against bots on one device first, then friends over Wi-Fi and DS local wireless. It's an original game in the hex resource-trading genre, not a copy of an existing one. The design, with the reasoning behind each decision, is in [design.md](design.md).

It's built on SAKBox's libraries, as its own project: the [hex grid](../hex_grid/README.md), [seeded random numbers](../seeded_random/README.md), the [action log](../action_log/README.md) and [game bots](../game_bots/README.md), and later [ScratchPadd](../scratchpadd/README.md) for its screens and networking.

## The rules

The rules engine's first version is the one in [design.md](design.md#the-rules-engines-first-version-2026-11-15). In short: ships explore a hex board of face-down planets; turning a planet over earns an explorer badge, and settling it makes a station that collects its resource when its number is rolled; stations become colonies; contracts are secret goals; first to 10 points on their own turn wins.

How the code is laid out (`rules/include/CosmicHex/Rules.hpp`):

- **`Settings`:** every number a rule uses, in one table: points to win, ship moves, costs, trade rates, how many of each tile, the dice numbers, the zone's finds. It's part of the state, so an action log replays under the numbers it was played with, and simulations can try other numbers without touching the code.
- **`State`:** the board (planets, the deep-space zone, ships), each player's resources, badges and contracts, what's left to draw, and where in a turn the game is (`Phase`).
- **`Action`:** everything a player or the dealer does: choosing the home's resource, the dealer's roll, moving the blockade, trading, building, moving a ship, the dealer turning a tile over, settling, colonies, contracts, cashing badges, ending the turn.
- **`Game`:** the rules for the [action log](../action_log/README.md) and the [bots](../game_bots/README.md): `apply` (an action, or false if the rules don't allow it), `legalActions`, whose turn it is, the dealer's draws, scoring, and the text a log keeps.
- **`newGame(players, settings)`**, **`points`**, and the **contracts** (`contracts()`: each a goal checked against the board and the player's tokens, and its points).

Two choices make the rules simple to reason about:

- **Face-down tiles aren't laid out at the start.** A tile is drawn from what's left of its pile when a ship turns it over, by the dealer. That's the same as a shuffled face-down tile, but nothing hidden sits on the board, so the state never holds a secret a bot could peek at.
- **The only hidden information is the contracts in each hand.** A bot's guess (`determinize`) deals the other players' contracts again from the ones it hasn't seen.

## Simulating

```sh
bazel run -c opt //projects/cosmic_hex/simulate -- --games 200 --players 4 --iterations 100
```

It reports how often each seat wins (turn order's advantage), how many rounds a game lasts, where the winners' points came from (stations, colonies, Core Control, badges, contracts), and how many games didn't finish. Its defaults are the rules as agreed (and since): no Core Control (`--core-control yes` to bring it back), no fuel boost in the first round, corners and edges, the deep-space start, the home's income on the home die (2 a turn below 5 points, the home flipping to a planet at 5), the Imperial Tithe at 7, free stations (the ship costing minerals, fuel and water), finishing the round (`--finish-round no` to end at once), `--first-boost yes` (a fuel boost in the first round, as the rules once had; the agreed rule has none), trading between players and finds beside the small systems; turn each off with `--edges no`, `--start home`, `--home-die no`, `--catch-up-below 0`, `--exhaust 0`, `--discard-over 0`, `--trading no`, `--finds usual`. Flags: `--games`, `--players` (2-4), `--iterations` (the bots' thinking: simulated games a move), `--rollout` (how far each of those runs before it's judged), `--max-rounds` (where an unfinished game is stopped), `--first random` (a random player goes first, each keeping their home, to tell turn order and home apart), `--seat-bonus a,b,c,d` (extra start resources for the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th to play), `--empty N` (empty tiles of the zone's 12, 5 by default, the rest shared between salvage and ruins after the outposts and the pirate hideout), `--outposts N` (alien outposts among them, 2 by default), `--station-cost` and `--ship-cost` (five numbers, minerals, food, fuel, water, crystals: `--station-cost 0,0,0,0,0` for free stations), `--kinds` (how common each kind of planet is, five weights: `--kinds 1,1,2,1,1` for gas giants twice as common), `--exhaust N` (the exhausted home: at N points a player's home's chosen resource is its last, and it becomes a planet of that kind on 4, 5, 9 or 10, paying everyone by the dice; `--exhaust-kind random` draws its kind, an equal chance of each, instead), `--cartel all|N` (the Interstellar Cartel takes all of the named resource from each player, or up to N), `--finish-round yes` (reaching the points to win plays out the round, so everyone has had as many turns; the most points wins, a tie to the later in turn order), `--home-steps 0,2,5,1` (the home's income in steps by points, from,amount pairs: here 2 a turn, then 1 from 5 points, as Starfarers' reserve pile; it takes the place of `--catch-up-below`), `--home-die yes` (the home's resources rolled on a six-sided die, one face per resource and the sixth the player's choice; with `--exhaust 5`, the agreed hybrid), `--trading no` (no trading between players; it's on by default: after the roll the player whose turn it is offers trades to the others, the bots by the trading rule, `CosmicHex/TradingRule.hpp`), `--exhaust N` (the exhausted home: at N points a player's home's chosen resource is its last, and it becomes a planet of that kind on 4, 5, 9 or 10, paying everyone by the dice), `--strong ITERATIONS` (one stronger bot, at a different home each game, to check skill tells), `--variant LABEL` (a label for the CSV), `--catch-up-below N` and `--catch-up-gap N` (a player behind takes an extra resource with their home's income), `--discard-over N` (a hand limit: on a 7, a player holding more than N resources gives back half, from what they hold most of), `--skip-first yes` (the first player goes without their first home income), `--core seven|three` (the core planets: the 7 within 2 of the centre, or the smaller core: the centre and one opposite pair, or for three players the centre and the three a third of the way round), `--planets q,r,q,r...` and `--homes q,r,...` (a layout's planets and homes, such as the cluster boards, instead of a planet every third hex and the usual homes), `--edges yes` (corners and edges, design.md: stations on corners, ships moving along edges, 2 a turn and 1 more for fuel unless `--move N` and `--boost N` say otherwise), `--start deep-space` (with `--edges yes`: each player's starting ship goes on any corner touching no planet, placed in reverse order, turning over the finds it lands by), `--choose-home no` (each seat's home fixed by the board; by default each player chooses their home at the start, in turn order, putting their home colony on a corner of any home not yet taken), `--layout NAME` (a board from `layouts/layouts.txt`: the layouts R1-R7 and S1-S6, each kind of system the same moves from every home, and E1-E17 and K1-K19; `--layouts FILE` for another file; the finds go beside the layout's singles and pairs, unless `--finds usual`), `--zone q,r,...` (where the deep-space finds are, instead of the usual 12 places), `--moons N` (moons among the face-down planets: a badge when turned over, no resource, can hold a station), `--win N` (points to win; the rules' default is 12), `--relay-points N` (what the Deep-space relay Charter itself scores once revealed, on top of its station's 1; the rules' default is 1), `--dominion N` (Imperial Dominion's points: to whoever has the most colonies on other players' former homes; 0, off, by default), `--charters a,b,c` (the Charters in the deck, by their number in `contracts()`; 21,19,20 by default: stations on 3 planets of one kind, Occupier, the relay; 0 is the older 3 kinds of planet), `--strong N --others random` (one bot thinking N, the others choosing any legal move at random: does thinking beat chance?), `--styles plain,saver,rusher,explorer` (bot styles, `CosmicHex/BotStyles.hpp`: the same search with the bot's own rollout moves leaning to saving, building at once or exploring; player p plays the ((p + game) mod count)th, so each style plays every home and turn position, and the summary gives each style's wins), `--seed`, `--logs FOLDER` to keep every game's action log for replaying or study, and `--csv FILE` for one line a game (its variant, seed, who went first, which seat had the stronger bot, the winner's seat, rounds, actions, everyone's points, where the winner's came from, and what the blockade, catching up and the hand limit did, the players' styles, the stations on singles, pairs and threes at the end, and every badge earned), to combine runs and analyse them; beside it, `FILE.contracts.csv` says how often each contract was dealt, kept, revealed, and revealed by the winner. Every game has its own seed, made from `--seed` and the game's number (so runs with different `--seed`s never repeat a game); it's in the game's log header and its CSV line.

## On the 3DS

`bazel run //projects/cosmic_hex/3ds:cosmic-hex-3ds` builds the 3DS app (Docker must be running: see `tools/devkitpro`) and opens it in Azahar; `tools/fbi_install` installs it on a 3DS. The screens (`ScreenFlow`): the title (the title scene on top, Play and Quit below); Play opens the setup, its settings on the touch screen (`StartMenu`, drawn by `MenuPicture`): the players (four, you and three bots, or three, you and two), the board (`boardChoices()` for that many players, the default first: R3 for four, T4 for three; the four-cluster boards FR1, FR3 and FS1 last, the top screen noting their longer games), how it ends (finish the round, or the first to the target wins at once), R3's seat bonus (off unless chosen), and the mode (you play, or watch the bots), with the board chosen on the top screen, sliding out for the next one on the dice's curve (speeding up as the dice's glass does when it's pulled away, then slowing the same way: `slideAcross`, ScratchPadd's Motion library's `Pull` curve); Start begins the game, Back returns to the title. A game starts with who goes first: each player rolls a die, those tied for the highest rolling again (`TableGame::firstRoll`), shown over the board with the placing order, for 4 seconds or until A. Then the start's placing: in turn order each player chooses a home not yet taken and puts their home colony on one of its corners, then, back again, their ship on a corner in open space; on your turn the places are marked, a touch shows the piece there, and End Turn (or A) places it (B, or touching it again, takes it back). In a game START opens the pause menu (Resume, Restart, Title, Quit); once someone wins, the title scene comes back with who won. Touch or the buttons work everywhere; the title scene is ScratchPadd's `title_scene` (the title in blocky outlined letters, the rocket from `models/` turning on its hexagonal pedestal, the night sky turning slowly). On your move the moves you can make are listed on the top screen: touch the board to list those at a corner (`MoveSpots`: a touch picks the corner nearest it, and after a 7 the blockade's hex), up and down to choose, A to make it, B to list them all again: the board in 3D on the top screen (stereo with the 3D slider), each player's points as a row of dashes at the top (`PointsBars`: a dash a point in their colour, grey to the target, the turn's row outlined in turquoise) and the last move in words over it. Each tile shows its face (`art/`: SVG drawings the build turns into texture recipes, drawn into textures when the game starts), and a turned-over planet its number on a round chip with a dot for each way two dice make it (`art/make_number_chips.py` writes the chips' SVGs). The board screen's button icons are `art/icons/*.svg` and the cards' drawings `art/cards/*.svg` (swap in your own by replacing a file). A card's back is `art/cards/back_rings.svg` or `back_planets.svg` (the six kinds of planet on a starfield for resource cards, rings of hexagons for action cards). The cards' words are `art/cards/cards.txt` (a line a card, its title, short title and what it does; the build embeds it and `Art::wordsOf` reads it; `Art_test` checks every card's words fit, in the consoles' font). A card's drawing has no words: the game writes the title (centred, up to two lines) and what it does over it, in the areas `Art.hpp` gives (`kCardTitle`, `kCardArt`, `kCardWords`, in the drawing's 63 x 88 units), so a new drawing keeps to them. Ships slide from corner to corner and planets and finds flip over when explored (`BoardMotion`), over a sparse starfield. In a game the bottom screen is `BottomScreen`: the board with its buttons (`BoardButtons`: Show resources and the other players top left; Build, Trade and Cards down the top right; Roll bottom left, End turn bottom right, each only when it can be used; the icons `art/icons`), and in its place the choosing screens (`ChoiceScreens`, drawn on a `Canvas`): the build screen, the resource picker (`Pickers`: the home's pay and salvage open it by themselves; the Cartel and the Supply drop will from the cards screen) and the player picker (stealing, when the blockade stands by more than one player). It decides from the rules' legal moves; its taps it doesn't use select a corner as before. Show resources, the other players, Trade and Cards don't open anything yet. The bottom screen shows the board flat (`BoardPicture`: the same faces, the pieces as shapes): touch to select the corner nearest the touch (a small ring on both screens, not the whole tile; the hex, outlined, when moving the blockade), drag to move the board when zoomed in, X held with R and L to zoom in and out (L and R alone are left and right on a choosing screen). Zooming shows at once: the whole board's faces, kept, scaled up, sharpening a face a frame as the zoom's own are drawn. The map draws only what changed since it was last drawn (each piece listed with its look and area, the areas where pieces came, went or changed drawn again from scratch; the buttons' areas drawn again under them), and only the rows that changed are copied to the screen; BoardPicture_test checks it matches drawing it all, over whole games. Each roll is thrown with the physics dice (`dice/`, below) on the top screen, in front of the board (`DicePieces`: the dice's mesh and the glass container placed facing the camera): the game waits (`TableGame`'s `physicsDice`) until they settle, plays the faces they show (the roll shown in large words in the middle of the screen on a hexagon), and the container is pulled off the screen, the roll going with it. The dice are 1.2 across, grey, their corners rounded (a chamfered cube, 44 triangles and the pips). The setup shows the board from straight above, lit flat like the map; on Start it comes down to the game's view on the dice's curve while the bottom screen fades from the menu to the map. The simulator and the balance lab run the bots with a frame arena of 128 KB too (`--arena 0` for none): about 18% faster on a Mac, the same games. The -watch builds also show where each frame's time goes (FrameProfile: the bottom screen, its copy to the screen, the dice, the top screen and its wait for the refresh, the board's pieces, the renderer's CPU time and its overlay's, and the bottom screen's map and buttons). A pass on the DS measured with it: the text drawn in whole numbers (the overlay's worst frame 40 ms to 8), fewer divides in placing each mesh, the board's places kept, colours word-aligned and the bottom screen copied a word at a time (its worst frame about 100 ms to 45); quiet seconds went from 23 to 33 frames a second to about 55. On the DS the map's tile faces are drawn with coarser smoothing (2 x 2 samples), one a frame during the title and setup screens. The DS steps the dice's physics 30 times a second (60 elsewhere): just as fair (dice_fairness_flex_point16 over 20,000 throws: faces chi-square 1.63, sums 11.28; `dice_fairness -- THROWS STEPS_A_SECOND`), and rolls run at 22 to 26 frames a second there rather than 8 to 14. The rules are tuned for the bots' search (measured with the -watch builds' search breakdown and the Mac's sampler, every simulated game unchanged): ships' reach tabled at start, a hex index in State for what's on a hex (made by newGame), the blockade's owners and points in one pass over the stations; the simulator is 2.4 times as fast. Their small hot helpers are marked `[[gnu::hot]]`, which the DS build puts in ITCM. On the DS an unchanged top screen isn't drawn again: the 3D hardware shows its last frame by itself, and the time goes to the bots (drawing a still board each frame left them 4% of a DS's CPU; in melonDS they went from about 25 games a move to about 130). The bots search with a frame arena of 128 KB (GameBots' `frameArenaBytes`), and the -watch builds show how many games a move they play. With ScratchPadd's counting allocator in the build, the app measures its memory every 2 seconds (the heap, and allocations a second by padd), logged in a debug build and shown on the top screen in the -watch builds. Moves animate and the dice roll on the game's own clock (ScratchPadd's `GameClock`), which stops while a watched game is paused; dice still moving after 8 seconds are thrown again (a watchdog). A pauses or goes on, START quits.

The app is `handheld/source/cosmic_hex_on_a_handheld.cpp`. What it plays and draws is in `handheld/` and tested on the Mac (`TableGame`: seats for people and bots, the dealer, the action log, each move in words; `BoardPieces`: where hexes and corners are, and the pieces' meshes and colours; `BoardMotion`: what moved between two moves, part way; `BoardView`: the top screen's cameras; `BuildMenu`: the build screen's rows, each buildable or red with why, from the rules' own legal moves). The board's thin layers (the number chips, the chosen hex's ring) sit high enough, and the cameras' near plane far enough out, for the DS's coarse depth buffer, and the grid between the tiles lies only in the gaps: `BoardView_test` checks them against a model of that buffer (the far find tiles had lost triangles to the grid under them). The rules it plays are the rules library's `agreedRules()`, which the simulator's defaults are tested to match.


## On the DS

`bazel run //projects/cosmic_hex/ds:cosmic-hex-ds` builds the DS app and opens it in melonDS: the same game and app as the 3DS's (one source, `handheld/source/cosmic_hex_on_a_handheld.cpp`, the differences in `#if` blocks), its bots thinking less (no floating-point hardware), the tiles' faces as 32-pixel textures (the DS's VRAM is small), and the flat board on the bottom screen (256 x 192, a 16-bit bitmap in VRAM bank C). On both consoles, the `-watch` builds (`cosmic-hex-ds-watch`, `cosmic-hex-3ds-watch`) start watching four bots by themselves, for unattended runs.

## The layout solver

`bazel run -c opt //projects/cosmic_hex/solver:solve_layouts -- --threes 2 --away 3 --farther yes` searches for boards that meet the constraints you set and prints the best as lines of a layouts file (add them to `layouts/layouts.txt` and simulate with `--layout NAME`). The centre is like any other hex unless you ask for a lone planet there (`--centre lone`: no Core Control, so nothing needs a planet at the middle): any cluster may cover it, or nothing. Counts of singles, pairs, threes, fours (a compact diamond of four: `--fours 2`) and finds are each a value or a floor-ceiling range (`--threes 2`, `--pairs 1-3`); homes on the edge (as the R layouts) or the corners (`--homes corners`, as the S); clusters never touch each other, the core or a home, and the board is the same seen from opposite homes. The menu so far: `--away N` (finds never touch a cluster of N or more), `--farther yes` (from every home, the nearest three, and four, is farther than the nearest pair and single), `--nearest-single`, `--nearest-pair`, `--nearest-three`, `--nearest-four A-B` (from every home, that cluster within A to B ship moves), and `--reach-moves D --in-reach A-B` (every home has A to B planets within D moves), and `--same-near D` (every home has the same number of planets within each distance up to D moves: unequal near planets were found to make a board unfair where equal access overall didn't show it). Distances are a ship's moves along edges from a home's corners. Results come ranked by how equal every home's access to planets is (planets weighted by 1 / (1 + moves)). The library is `solver/` (`LayoutSolver.hpp`), tested on its own.

## Tests

`bazel test //projects/cosmic_hex/...`

`Rules_test` checks:
- the board (19 planets, 4 homes, 7 core planets, the 12 zone tiles, with and without the corners variant) and the start;
- a turn's order;
- production, with colonies paying double and a blockaded planet paying nothing;
- the blockade on a 7, and its theft;
- moving up to 3 hexes, or 5 for a fuel, stopping on a face-down tile, which the dealer turns over, with a badge;
- settling, colonies, and at most two stations a planet;
- Core Control;
- contracts bought, dealt, kept and revealed;
- badges cashed in;
- the bank's 4 for 1;
- the zone's salvage and pirates;
- winning at 10 on your own turn;
- the other players' contracts hidden from a bot;
- the text of the starting state and of every action;
- two bots playing a whole game to a winner, whose action log replays exactly.

## Dice

`dice/` throws the two dice in Cyclone: the top screen is a glass container (a floor, walls at the screen's edges, a lid), two dice are thrown in from the left and the faces they settle on are the roll; then the container is pulled off the screen, the dice still simulated and sliding on its floor. A seed decides the throw, so the same seed throws the same on the same machine. `bazel run -c opt //projects/cosmic_hex/dice:dice_fairness -- 20000` throws them many times and checks each face comes up a sixth of the time and the sums match two fair dice (chi-square); `dice_fairness_float` and `dice_fairness_flex_point16` do the same in the 3DS's float and the DS's 16.16 fixed point.

## Models

`models/title_models.blend` holds the title screen's rocket, a placeholder made by `models/make_title_models.py` (run it with Blender: `blender --background --factory-startup --python make_title_models.py -- title_models.blend`). To use your own, open the `.blend` in Blender and change or replace the object named `Rocket`; the build exports and cooks it for each console (`tools/assets`), simplifying the DS's version to its budget if it needs to. The console apps need Blender to build (tagged `blender`).
